## Method and limits

- **Step 1 (free):** Agent > All Content > Filters > Platform: Reels returned **56 Reels**. The "Face" filter offers Faceless / Face on screen, but it only covers videos Virlo has analysed, and it returned **1 Reel** (@finntonry). **There is no Hands/POV filter.** The Format filter's options are Talking Head, B Roll Montage, Activity Demonstration, Slideshow Text, Product Closeup and others. All 56 Reels are listed in the appendix.
- **Step 2 (paid): not run.** Agents > New Agent with your exact intent produced 10 keywords and 9 exclusions. The quoted cost was **150 credits** for a one-time search, the same with 1 keyword or 10. A recurring agent is also 150 per weekly update. The setup has **no platform selector**: it searches TikTok, Shorts, Reels and Meta Ads together. I closed the setup without launching. No recurring monitor was created.
- **How the Reels were reviewed:** for each Reel I sampled the video every 0.25 to 0.5 seconds and counted big frame-to-frame changes to estimate cuts, then grabbed 12 frames (0s, 0.5s, 1.2s, 2s, then evenly spaced) to read the on-screen text and check for faces. Cut counts are **rough**, because camera motion inflates them. Video was muted, so **whether the clip's own sound is kept is not shown** for any Reel. Song names come from the Instagram audio label.
- **Shares:** Virlo shows 0 shares for every Reel because Instagram doesn't expose them. The Instagram page does show a **repost count**, which I've used as the closest share-type signal.

## 2. The five patterns these Reels share

1. **First second: the finished dish, already being eaten or handled.** [Data] 7 of 9 open on the end result with a hand doing something to it: dipping, lifting, pulling apart. The process comes after. Exceptions: petez.eatz opens on an empty pot, and thehumblepescatarian on the raw grocery flat-lay. [Inference] Lead with the payoff, then show how.
2. **Text: small, lowercase, centered, no background box, plus a series label.** [Data] @recipeincaption and @ashieeeats use small white lowercase text mid-frame. @hangrywife and thehumblepescatarian use a centered serif. Four Reels name a series within 2 seconds ("episode 53:", "episode 1", "Tins Made Tasty"). The **cover text is the dish name and differs from the first-frame text**: the covers read "dumpling lasagna" and "korean popcorn chicken," while the videos open on "dinners you'll actually make."
3. **Pacing: a new shot every 1 to 2 seconds, about 1 per second in the sub-10-second Reels.** [Data, rough automated count] Both 9-second Reels (hangrywife, petez.eatz) change shots about once a second. The 30 to 60 second Reels run about one change every 1.5 to 2 seconds.
4. **Audio: voice does most of the work.** [Inference from captions] 6 of 9 show running subtitle captions, the signature of a voiceover. 3 of 9 carry a licensed song [Data]. Only @hangrywife is pure music with a single title card, and only @petez.eatz has no text at all.
5. **Endings: loop to the money shot, or hand off to an app.** [Data] Four end on the finished dish so the loop restarts cleanly (hangrywife, dumpling lasagna, petez.eatz, thehumblepescatarian). Four end on, or spend their last third in, an app screen recording (@recipeincaption ×3, @ashieeeats). Two end on the creator's face. Caption CTAs: the **"Comment "Recipe"" DM keyword** on 5 of 9 (all @recipeincaption, plus @petez.eatz). The **full recipe in the caption** on 3 (hangrywife, thehumblepescatarian, ashieeeats).

## 6. Where this contradicts the first audit

1. **"Sound barely matters": only half true for your format.** [Data] No sound is shared here either, so no single sound drives the niche. But the one Reel in your exact format leans on a licensed pop song, and most of the others lean on a voiceover [Inference]. For a no-voice account with cleared music only, audio is the weakest part of the format. [Inference] Test an AI voiceover against music-only before you commit to music-only.
2. **"Fridge rescue is the open angle": supported, but small.** [Data] @petez.eatz ("pantry clean out") and @hangrywife ("items in your pantry and fridge") are the two small accounts beating their size. The higher-volume faceless format on Instagram is "dinners you'll actually make" plus an app demo, and Osta already runs it. [Inference] Fridge rescue is still the angle no app owns. Osta owns the recipe-plus-app-demo Reel.
3. **"App pitches don't get shared": contradicted on Instagram.** [Data] @recipeincaption's recipe-then-app Reels have the highest Instagram repost counts in the set (18.3K, 24.3K, 25.1K, 37.3K). The first audit's finding came from TikTok share counts on confession-style app ads. [Inference] An app demo works when it comes after a real recipe, as its last third, not as the whole video.
4. **"Longer videos lean better": not supported for faceless Reels.** [Data] The top Virality score (43) is a 9.8-second Reel, and the 8.8-second @petez.eatz beats its size too. The 30 to 40 second @recipeincaption Reels also do well. With 9 Reels, length isn't a reliable signal either way. Treat audit 1's length finding as TikTok-only.

## What I'd make from this

One format, tested in two audio versions:

- **0 to 2s:** the finished dish, with a hand dipping or lifting it, plus a serif title naming the dish and its time ("12 Minute Fridge Fried Rice").
- **2 to 18s:** hands-only process, one shot per second, starting from the fridge or receipt items.
- **Last 4 to 6s:** a real iEatz screen recording turning those items into the recipe. Use only verified real app screens.
- **Then:** loop back to the dish.
- **Caption:** full recipe, plus a "Comment RECIPE" DM CTA.
- **The test:** version A with a cleared music track only, version B with an AI voiceover over the same cut.

This shows whether your no-voiceover constraint is costing you anything. [Inference]
